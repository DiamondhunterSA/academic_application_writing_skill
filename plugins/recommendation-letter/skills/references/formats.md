# Recommendation Formats and Evidence Handling

## Full Academic Letter

Use the supplied prompt, audience, and space limit to set the structure. A useful letter often develops the following elements in connected paragraphs.

1. A clear recommendation and an explanation of the recommender's relationship to the applicant, including the setting and duration when known.
2. A substantive example showing performance in work the recommender taught, supervised, assessed, or discussed.
3. Another observation that deepens the evaluation or demonstrates development, judgment, communication, or collaboration.
4. An assessment of readiness for the opportunity, followed by a recommendation consistent with the recommender's supplied stance.

Choose length according to the evidence and requested constraints. A short relationship may support a focused letter. Additional examples should earn their space through a distinct contribution to the evaluation.

Use the addressee supplied. A general admissions committee salutation is suitable when no individual recipient is known. Include a date, address block, or subject only when appropriate to the requested format. Use plain supplied contact details and a typed name/title block for the draft.

When the user requests a finished Word or PDF artifact, use the available document tools to create and inspect it. Institutional letterhead, signature images, and other supplied assets need authorization for the requested use. Complete the prose while any such assets remain unresolved.

## Online Referee Questions

Map each portal question to the relevant evidence and answer it directly within its own limit. Common questions concern the relationship, academic performance, research potential, comparisons, strengths, and development.

- Establish the relationship in the requested field using the actual capacity, duration, and setting.
- Support strengths or potential with a specific observed example and the judgment it informs.
- For comparisons, preserve the stated reference group, period, and basis. If a ranking or rating requires a judgment the recommender has not supplied, draft the evidence-based prose and flag that field for completion.
- If the prompt requests a weakness or area for development, use a supplied observation and any documented response to feedback. Ask for the missing observation when needed.
- For separate fields, allocate evidence deliberately and make each answer understandable on its own. Preserve consistency in factual claims and strength of evaluation.

## Evidence and Evaluation

Use these distinctions while planning and reviewing the letter.

| Available material | How it can support the draft |
|---|---|
| An applicant's account of shared work or the recommender's own notes | Draft the corresponding first-person observation within the supplied relationship and scope, with recommender review indicated outside an applicant-prepared draft |
| A supplied grade, assessment record, or documented result | Preserve its scale, unit, status, date, and individual or team scope |
| A CV item outside the relationship described | Attribute it if relevant, ask how the recommender knows it, or omit it from the letter |
| A specific action and result | Propose a reasoned evaluation of the demonstrated quality, calibrated to the episode |
| A supplied comparative judgment | Preserve the actual comparison group, timeframe, and degree of endorsement |
| Conflicting accounts of contribution or outcome | Ask a focused factual question and draft the remaining supported content |

Selecting a recommender role establishes the writing perspective. Personal contact, achievements, and evaluative details still come from the supplied facts and proposed judgments grounded in those facts.

## Calibrated Paragraph Examples

All details below are fictional illustrations. They supply no facts about the applicant or recommender.

### Research Judgment With an Inconclusive Result

Supplied material says that a research mentor met weekly with an applicant on a sensor-modeling project. The applicant found leakage in a split, rebuilt evaluation, and explained why the original gain disappeared. The manuscript remains under review.

> In our weekly discussions of the sensor-modeling project, Lin identified that our initial data split allowed information to pass between training and evaluation sets. Lin rebuilt the evaluation and explained why the apparent improvement disappeared under the corrected procedure. I valued the care with which Lin examined an attractive result and made its limitations clear. This work gives me confidence in Lin's developing judgment as a researcher.

The paragraph supports an assessment through the documented response to a methodological problem. Describe the manuscript as under review if its status is relevant elsewhere in the letter.

### Teaching Evidence With a Known Grade

Supplied material says that an instructor taught an applicant for one semester. The applicant received 92/100, revised an essay to address a counterargument, and discussed that revision in office hours. No cohort rank is supplied.

> I taught Lin in Research Methods during the spring semester. In an essay on causal inference, Lin responded to my feedback by addressing a competing explanation and revising the argument around the available evidence. Our discussion in office hours showed a willingness to test the reasoning behind a conclusion. Lin earned 92 out of 100 in the course, and the revised essay demonstrated the analytical care I would expect in advanced study.

The course grade and the observed revision support the evaluation. A numerical rank would require additional comparative information.

## Immediate Drafts With Gaps

When the user asks for a draft now, produce the complete requested format using the supported relationship and examples. Use a few descriptive placeholders for essential details such as [Course and term] or [Recommender title]. Keep a short completion note outside the letter.

Prioritize the missing relationship or an observation that would anchor a major evaluative paragraph. If optional information such as cohort ranking is unavailable, write the supported evaluation in ordinary prose. Treat a newly supplied mentor judgment as input for the next revision.

## Several Letters and Target Variants

For letters from several recommenders, organize the working evidence by recommender, relationship, observed episode, principal judgment, and supplied voice preferences. Produce a complete letter for each requested recommender.

Use relationship-specific details to shape the letters. A project supervisor may explain a design decision, a research mentor may assess experimental reasoning, and an instructor may evaluate an argument or revision. The shared applicant facts should agree wherever they recur.

For letters to several targets, reuse the confirmed relationship and observations while adapting the program name, opportunity, criteria, and relevant assessment. Label the drafts clearly outside the letter and check each version's names and constraints.
