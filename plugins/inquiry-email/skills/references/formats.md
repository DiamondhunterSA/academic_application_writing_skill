# Inquiry Email Format Differences

This guide covers differences in material selection, emphasis, and delivery. Use the shared Gather → Plan → Write → Polish → Iterate workflow and [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) for common mechanics, factual checks, attachments, and thread handling. The actual opportunity, recipient's instructions, and conversation take priority over these categories.

## 1. Initial Contact with a Prospective Supervisor

For a PhD supervision inquiry, choose the emphasis and information order that serve the user's preference and purpose. Both directions below can be used for a first contact. They do not prescribe email length or distinguish an initial inquiry from a follow-up; both must make the contact purpose clear and give the recipient enough context to answer.

### Background-and-fit

Briefly introduce relevant background, a project, or a capability, explain its supported connection to the supervisor's work, and then make the request. Select what helps the recipient assess the proposed connection rather than summarizing the whole CV. This direction can remain concise, with the general purpose apparent before the final question.

### Purpose-first

State the purpose or main question early, with the minimum identity, research direction, and intended intake needed to make it answerable. A project account or research-fit argument may be omitted when it does not help answer the question. Keep essential context even in a brief first inquiry.

### Choosing the Direction

- Follow the user's current explicit choice and honor the recipient's stated contact requirements.
- Otherwise, use any applicable, previously confirmed preference together with the current goal and thread. Choose directly when these provide enough basis. A rich CV or a lack of material does not establish the user's preferred direction.
- If the direction is still unclear, the difference matters, and a clarification suits the user's request, ask one focused preference question rather than restarting intake.
- Provide both directions when the user requests a comparison. You may also provide both when the user wants a direct result, both are reasonable, and the user has not limited the output to one version. Use the same confirmed facts and core request in both; change emphasis and order without inventing extra evidence or a different goal.
- When the user requests one version and has not chosen a direction, make a reasonable choice, briefly state the assumption, and proceed. Do not require two versions or separate outline approval.
- If the user wants preferences saved, record only the confirmed preference and the situations where it applies. Do not turn a choice for one inquiry into a universal preference.

Ask the question appropriate to the available information, such as whether the recipient expects to consider students in that area or whether the proposed direction might fit the group. If recruitment instructions already explain the process, follow them rather than requesting an exception or treating an encouraging reply as an admission offer.

## 2. RA, Internship, or Visiting Opportunity

Name an advertised opportunity when there is one; otherwise make clear that the sender is asking about a possible placement. Prioritize relevant skills or contributions, intended dates, duration, and practical availability. Include status, institutional arrangements, or funding needs when they affect the placement and the user has supplied them.

Distinguish among employment, an internship, and a visiting affiliation rather than treating them as equivalent. Ask about the suitable route, availability, or next step without assuming a salary, visa arrangement, host commitment, or permission to work. Do not offer unpaid work or other concessions on the user's behalf.

## 3. Admissions Eligibility and Procedures

Identify the program and application cycle, then state the specific qualification, material, or procedural issue. Include the relevant published wording or a concise link when it helps explain an ambiguity, discrepancy, or case not covered by the instructions.

Ask for a clarification the office can give, such as whether a particular qualification is accepted or which submission route applies. Keep research motivation and personal background to what is necessary for that question; do not ask for an admission prediction or imply that eligibility guarantees selection.

## 4. Funding and Scholarship Inquiry

Identify the award, funding scheme, or type of support when known. Separate questions about eligibility, coverage, conditions, deadlines, and application procedures from questions about a supervisor's project funding. Direct each question to the person or office responsible for it.

Include only the sender's circumstances needed to answer the inquiry. Do not invent financial hardship, assume an award is need-based, or combine a procedural email with an unsolicited scholarship essay. Ask about the applicable funding route or confirmed terms rather than implying that support is already available or guaranteed.

## 5. Work or Research Collaboration Inquiry

Identify the role, shared question, project, or practical need that makes contact relevant. Explain a concrete potential contribution and the evidence supporting it, with enough context for the recipient to judge whether further discussion would be useful.

For a job-related inquiry, follow any vacancy or application instructions and distinguish asking about an opportunity from submitting a full application. For research collaboration, suggest a bounded question or initial discussion rather than assuming shared authorship, resources, access, or an agreed project. Do not send a complete proposal unless requested.

## 6. Follow-Up After No Reply

Use the existing thread when possible and briefly identify the unanswered question. Include a meaningful update only when there is one. Avoid resending a full introduction, adding pressure, or implying that the recipient owes a response.

Judge whether and when to follow up from the actual sent date, stated response window, relevant deadline, prior follow-ups, and recipient's instructions; there is no universal interval. Mention a genuine deadline factually if it affects the request. Do not keep pursuing a closed opportunity, a rejection, or a request for no further contact.

## 7. Continuing After a Reply

For an encouraging or conditional reply, respond to the concrete next step and any questions asked. Confirm only availability, materials, or commitments the user has supplied; interest in a discussion does not establish admission, employment, supervision, or funding.

For a clarification request or referral, provide the requested information or follow the suggested contact route. Preserve the original reply's conditions, and describe a referral accurately without implying endorsement. If the response is ambiguous, ask about the specific ambiguity rather than assuming agreement.

For a rejection or unavailable opportunity, an optional brief acknowledgement can close the exchange. Stop pursuing it unless the recipient explicitly invites a later inquiry or another route; a no-contact request requires no further message. Do not turn a refusal into a persuasion campaign.
