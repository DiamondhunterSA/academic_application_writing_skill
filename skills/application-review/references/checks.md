# Application Review Checks

Use the sections relevant to the supplied materials and the requested review. These are review criteria, not universal admissions requirements.

## 1. Establish Which Requirements Apply

- Associate each requirement with its target institution, program, application cycle, document or portal field, and source. Preserve the wording when a distinction such as words versus characters matters.
- Distinguish a current official requirement, an applicant-supplied instruction, and a reviewer recommendation. A convention or another program's instructions cannot establish a submission failure.
- Prefer clearly applicable, current program-specific instructions over general guidance. If official pages, a portal, and correspondence disagree and precedence is unclear, report the discrepancy rather than silently selecting a rule.
- Check whether an instruction is mandatory, optional, conditional, or a suggestion. Apply conditions such as degree track, funding route, and applicant category before judging compliance.
- Treat prompts by their actual questions. Document labels such as PS, SOP, and research statement do not have a universal meaning or interchangeable requirements.
- If a supplied URL cannot be accessed, use the provided excerpt with its provenance and identify the unresolved requirement. Do not fill gaps from memory.
- Use available official sources when current external facts need verification. Keep a record of the source and access date; do not imply a live portal or source was inspected when it was not.

An absent document is **missing from the reviewed set** until a confirmed applicable requirement and the applicant's submission plan establish that it is missing from the application.

## 2. Check Each Material's Intended Job

Judge coverage against the actual prompt and requested scope. Do not require every material below, or impose a fixed section order.

| Material | Main contribution to the application | Useful review questions |
|----------|--------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Academic CV / resume | A concise record of preparation, roles, outputs, and relevant experience | Are dates, roles, individual contributions, and output statuses clear? Does selection support this application? |
| SOP / academic motivation statement | An argument connecting preparation, academic interests, plans, and the opportunity | Are the requested questions answered through evidence rather than a CV rewritten as prose? |
| Personal history / background statement | The applicant's experiences, perspective, development, or contributions requested by the prompt | Does it add relevant understanding? Avoid requiring hardship, identity disclosure, or a dramatic origin story. |
| Research proposal | A specific research question and a defensible plan to investigate it | Are the question, rationale, methods, scope, and feasibility connected? Check additional components only when required. |
| Research statement | Prior contributions and a future research agenda, as requested | Is individual intellectual contribution clear, and does the future direction follow from the evidence? |
| Supplemental short answers | Direct answers to distinct questions under tight limits | Does each response answer its own question and add useful evidence to the set? |
| Writing sample | Evidence of the applicant's scholarly reasoning and writing | Is the chosen work relevant and compliant? Are excerpts, coauthorship, and personal contribution explained where needed? |
| Academic cover letter / inquiry email | A focused introduction or request suited to its recipient | Do opportunity, recipient, status, request, and attachment claims agree with the other materials? |
| Recommendation materials supplied by the user | Evidence that can support an independent recommender's account | Are cooperation dates, roles, and examples consistent? Do not request confidential letters or invent a recommender's judgments. |

## 3. Reconcile Facts Across Materials

Compare claims about the same entity or event before deciding that different wording represents a conflict.

- **Identity and affiliation:** applicant names, institution names, degree titles, departments, supervisor names, and official translations. Distinguish an accepted abbreviation from a different entity.
- **Dates and timelines:** study, employment, research, awards, and graduation dates. Distinguish expected from completed milestones. Overlapping activities alone do not prove an error.
- **Roles and contributions:** separate team outcomes from the applicant's work; distinguish assisting, implementing, designing, coordinating, and leading. Expanded detail or a documented role change can reconcile apparent differences.
- **Outputs and status:** keep in preparation, submitted, under review, accepted, and published distinct. A preprint does not establish acceptance. Check authorship, venue, title changes, and status dates before declaring incompatible claims.
- **Numbers:** compare units, denominators, evaluation sets, baselines, time periods, and rounding. A percentage-point change and a relative percentage improvement are different measures.
- **Qualifications:** preserve grading scales, ranks and their populations, language scores and dates, award level, and funding or admission status. Do not invent conversions or infer eligibility without the applicable rules.
- **Past versus planned work:** distinguish completed experiments, preliminary observations, planned methods, hoped-for collaborations, and career intentions. A plan is not evidence of a result or a confirmed arrangement.
- **Versions and languages:** check whether a newer confirmed fact explains an older draft. Translations may differ in phrasing but should preserve contribution, status, scale, and certainty. A filename containing "final" does not settle factual truth.

Use these distinctions when evaluating the evidence:

- **Confirmed conflict:** supplied claims are incompatible in the same relevant context. Identify both passages; confirmation that they disagree does not establish which is true.
- **Unverified claim:** the available material does not substantiate a claim. Missing corroboration is not proof that the claim is false.
- **Missing information:** a required answer, necessary clarification, source, or material is unavailable. State which kind is missing and what decision it prevents.

Repeated claims across documents are not independent verification. A CV is a useful comparison document, not an automatic source of truth. When the correct fact is unresolved, request confirmation and keep any proposed edit conditional.

## 4. Assess Coverage and Complementarity

- Map every substantive prompt question to a passage or a clear coverage gap. Do not count a keyword mention as an answer.
- Test whether a major claim about research readiness, leadership, impact, or motivation has a concrete example and an understandable link to the application.
- Distinguish useful factual continuity from repetitive explanation. The same project may supply a CV record, an SOP account of intellectual development, and a proposal's methodological foundation.
- Flag repetition when it consumes scarce space, leaves a requested question unanswered, or supplies the same takeaway across materials. Do not impose a universal overlap percentage or demand a different anecdote in every document.
- Preserve deliberate differences in emphasis across applications, degree tracks, and audiences. Different interests can coexist unless the statements make incompatible commitments or factual claims.
- Consider readability when an unexplained method, acronym, or project description prevents the intended reader from understanding the applicant's contribution. Suggest only the detail needed for that purpose.
- Address persuasive weaknesses through evidence selection, explanation, or reallocation before proposing additional achievements. Do not manufacture stronger outcomes, motivations, or promises.

## 5. Evaluate Program Fit and Research Logic

- Check target institution, program, degree track, intake, and recipient throughout the set. Look for leftovers from another application, including in salutations, headings, and filenames.
- For claims about faculty, labs, courses, facilities, and funding, distinguish a verified current fact from an inference about fit. Verify time-sensitive claims when tools and scope permit; otherwise identify them as unresolved.
- Test the connection between an opportunity and the applicant's preparation or intended work. Ranking, prestige, praise, and shared keywords alone do not explain that connection.
- Distinguish a faculty member's research relevance from availability to supervise, an advertised opening, a funding commitment, or permission to join a lab. One does not establish the others.
- Do not require named faculty, a lifelong ambition, a specific career destination, or a claim that a program is the only suitable option unless the actual prompt warrants it.
- In research materials, inspect the chain from prior work to the stated gap, question, method, expected evidence, and contribution. An interesting topic does not by itself establish a gap or a feasible method.
- Consider scale, timeline, access to data or materials, methodological preparation, and dependencies on collaborators or facilities. Identify consequential assumptions without requiring mature project certainty from an early-stage applicant.
- Check that citations support the associated claims and are bibliographically plausible. Do not claim to have verified a paper's contents from its title or an inaccessible reference.
- Calibrate disciplinary judgments to the evidence available. Phrase uncertain novelty, technical validity, or feasibility concerns as questions for investigation or specialist feedback rather than definitive findings.

## 6. Check Submission Constraints Within the Observed Scope

- **Length:** identify words, characters, pages, or another unit; the material included in the count; and the counting method. Count the actual reviewed version using suitable tools when available. Label estimates explicitly and do not present an estimated count as a confirmed violation.
- **Counting boundaries:** apply supplied rules for titles, headings, references, notes, spaces, and portal fields. Different counters may tokenize hyphens and mixed-language text differently; report material uncertainty near a limit.
- **Page limits and layout:** assess a rendered or viewable final document. Extracted text alone cannot confirm pagination, font size, margins, clipping, blank pages, or whether tables remain readable.
- **File constraints:** check actual filename, format, size, and content only when the artifact is accessible. A document named in a checklist is not evidence that it exists or is attached.
- **Completeness:** distinguish omitted required content from a file absent only from the reviewed set. Check visible unresolved placeholders, wrong targets, draft notes, and empty required sections.
- **Attachments and portal state:** reconcile statements such as "attached" with supplied files or confirmed intent. Do not report upload, delivery, portal acceptance, or recommendation submission as verified without observing the relevant state.
- **Deadlines:** preserve the specified date, time, timezone, and application route. Surface ambiguity where it affects the submission decision; do not assume the review environment's timezone is the institution's.

Conclude only within the reviewed scope: a textual review can identify no remaining textual findings while rendered files, external facts, required materials, or submission state remain unchecked.
