# Reporting an Application Review

## Priority and Evidence

Classify **priority** separately from **evidence status**. An unresolved check can be urgent without being a confirmed error.

| Priority | Use when | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| P1 — Address before submission | A confirmed requirements violation or material factual/attribution problem needs correction; or an unresolved issue could materially affect compliance or accuracy | Correct the identified text or verify the critical fact/rule |
| P2 — Strengthen the application | A supported weakness in prompt coverage, evidence, research reasoning, fit, or package complementarity reduces clarity or persuasiveness | Revise the affected passage or redistribute material |
| P3 — Optional refinement | A lower-impact clarity, organization, or style change would help | Apply if useful within the user's scope |

Use these evidence labels:

- **Confirmed:** directly observable in the supplied current materials or measured against an applicable requirement. For a contradiction, this confirms that the texts conflict, not which claim is true.
- **Needs verification:** the relevant fact, rule, version, or submission state cannot be established. Say what evidence would settle it.
- **Suggestion:** an editorial or strategic judgment, with its rationale. Do not present preferences as mandatory rules.

Lower the priority or omit a finding when its practical consequence is speculative. Do not turn an absent supporting document into a P1 issue unless its importance and applicability are established. Group duplicate symptoms under one finding when they share a correction.

## Adaptable Report

Use a concise report for a narrow request. For a full package, include the following elements without printing empty sections or unnecessary tables.

1. **Assessment and scope:** the most important outcome, target/cycle, materials and versions reviewed, and any material access limits. If only selected checks were requested, state that scope.
2. **Prioritized findings:** stable IDs such as `AR-01`, priority, evidence status, location, observation, consequence, and action. Put the highest-impact confirmed problems and urgent unresolved checks first.
3. **Requirement coverage:** show which applicable requirements were satisfied, not met, not assessable, or not applicable. For a long checklist, summarize satisfied requirements and list exceptions; distinguish "requirement unknown" from "requirement known but compliance unverified."
4. **Next actions:** list the smallest useful correction/verification sequence, with focused questions for unresolved facts. On recheck, identify resolved, open, and not-rechecked findings.

For each finding, provide enough detail to act:

| Field | What to include |
|---|---|
| Location | Filename or document label plus heading, paragraph, page, or line if observed; include both locations for conflicts |
| Evidence | Short exact excerpts, measured values with method, or the applicable rule with its specific source |
| Impact | Explain what becomes inaccurate, unanswered, noncompliant, or less convincing |
| Action | A concrete edit when facts are known, or the precise fact/source needed before editing |

Use a short excerpt as an anchor when page or line numbers are unavailable. Do not invent coordinates. Link local files or official sources when supported by the host. A large table is optional; findings can be clearer as short paragraphs.

End with a scope-limited status, for example:

- "Two material inconsistencies need correction before these drafts are ready."
- "No material problems found in the supplied CV and SOP; the portal requirements and remaining materials were not reviewed."
- "The revised claim is consistent in the CV and SOP. Its occurrence in the research proposal has not been rechecked."

Do not certify that unseen files, confidential references, or portal submissions are complete. Do not manufacture a fixed number of criticisms when the reviewed material has no substantive issues.

## Worked Examples

These examples are fictional. They illustrate review decisions, not facts about the user or any institution.

### Conflicting Publication Status

**AR-01 · P1 · Confirmed**

- **Location/evidence:** CV, "Publications": "Paper A — submitted"; SOP, research paragraph: "my accepted paper, Paper A." Both are supplied as current drafts; no acceptance confirmation is provided.
- **Impact:** The current package gives incompatible statuses for the same paper.
- **Action:** Confirm the current status and relevant date, then update every current occurrence. If it remains submitted, use "submitted manuscript"; if it was accepted after the CV draft, update the CV using the confirmed venue/status. The review does not establish which version is correct.

If the CV is explicitly a superseded draft, do not report a conflict within the current package. Point out an outdated file only if the applicant intends to submit it or the version choice remains unresolved.

### Team Result and Individual Contribution

**AR-02 · P1 · Needs verification**

- **Location/evidence:** CV, Project B: "Contributed data preprocessing to a four-person team"; SOP, Project B: "I independently developed the complete system."
- **Impact:** The descriptions may overstate individual responsibility or refer to different project phases.
- **Action:** Ask which components the applicant owned and whether the passages describe the same phase. Revise the wording around confirmed contributions; do not infer dishonesty or choose the more impressive account.

### Useful Recurrence Across Documents

The CV lists a project role, the SOP explains how that work shaped a research question, and a community essay describes mentoring teammates on the same project. These uses serve different prompts; do not flag them solely for sharing a project.

If the community prompt asks about contributions to others but repeats only technical results from the SOP, report the unanswered prompt component and recommend adding a relevant, real mentoring or community example. The problem is the missing answer, not a universal ban on repetition.

### Incomplete Package and Unknown Limits

Only a CV and SOP are supplied, with no program instructions. Review their consistency and evidence. State that required-material coverage, the SOP limit, and portal completion are not assessable. Do not invent a 1,000-word limit, require a research proposal, or mark recommendation letters missing.

### Narrow Revision Check

The user asks whether `AR-01` is fixed and supplies a revised CV only. Confirm the change in that file, compare with any available current SOP passage, and state which occurrences remain unreviewed. Do not announce that the entire application is ready or expand into a fresh style review.
