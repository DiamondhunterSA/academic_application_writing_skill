## Recommendation Letter

Produce a complete recommendation draft from the specified recommender's professional perspective. Follow Gather → Plan → Write → Polish → Iterate, starting at the stage supported by the supplied materials. An applicant may request a first-person draft for a mentor to review and adapt.

### Starting Point

- For a direct drafting request, use the available context and proceed once the relationship and usable evidence are clear. Ask focused questions or use a few explicit placeholders for essential gaps.
- For an existing letter, preserve its facts, intended recommendation strength, and requested editing scope. A translation or grammar edit should preserve the evaluator's judgment and qualifications.
- For an applicant-prepared draft, identify it as a draft for recommender review in a brief note outside the letter. When the recommender is the user, tailor the draft to their own account and requested voice.
- Use the actual recommender's name, title, relationship, and experience as supplied. Each first-person observation needs a basis in the shared experiences described. Attribute or omit background the recommender knows only through the applicant's materials.

### Stage 1 Gather

Establish who is recommending whom, for what opportunity, and on what basis.

1. Reuse the CV, transcript, shared project notes, existing recommendation, application prompt, and relevant context already provided. Existing applicant context files may help when they concern this applicant. Keep each recommender's identity and relationship distinguishable.
2. Establish the recommender's actual role and affiliation, how and when they knew the applicant, and what work they taught, supervised, assessed, or discussed. A day-to-day mentor, course instructor, and principal investigator may have different opportunities to observe the same work.
3. Identify the applicant's name and pronouns where known, target program or opportunity, degree or application type, language, requested format, and stated length or portal constraints. Preserve supplied forms of names and titles. Use neutral wording when pronouns remain unknown.
4. Gather a few concrete observations with their context, the applicant's own action, the outcome, and the judgment the episode supports. Useful material includes assessed work, design choices, research decisions, revisions after feedback, and contributions within a team.
5. Identify any supplied recommendation strength, reservations, comparative judgments, and voice preferences. Rankings require a known reference group, period, and basis. Grades and results need their scale or measurement context.
6. Ask at most 2-3 questions at once, concentrating on gaps that materially affect the letter. If the user requests an immediate draft, provide usable prose with focused placeholders or omit unsupported optional claims. Treat applicant accounts of shared work as draft material for the recommender to check.
7. Apply the actual prompt and requirements provided. Verify current institutional facts through available official sources when the task needs them. Record unresolved requirements outside the draft when verification is unavailable.

### Stage 2 Plan

Choose an evaluation the recommender can support through the relationship described.

1. Read the relevant role in [references/recommender-roles.md](references/recommender-roles.md). For combined roles, identify which setting supports each example.
2. Select a small number of consequential qualities supported by the evidence. Connect each quality to an observed episode and to the capabilities the application asks the referee to assess.
3. Track the basis for each proposed claim while planning. Distinguish shared-work observations, supplied records, applicant-reported background, and interpretations proposed for the recommender's review. Resolve conflicting dates, roles, results, or publication status before asserting a particular version.
4. Set the recommendation strength using the supplied evaluation and supporting evidence. A request for stronger writing can justify clearer judgments and better examples. Comparative rank, superlatives, and unconditional endorsements require the recommender's stated basis.
5. Use the requested format in [references/formats.md](references/formats.md). Show a brief outline when the user asks for planning or when a substantive choice needs discussion. Otherwise continue to the draft.

### Stage 3 Write

Write the requested letter or questionnaire response in the recommender's first person.

- Establish the recommendation, relationship, duration or setting where known, and basis of assessment early. Introduce professional credentials only when supplied and relevant.
- Develop selected examples through context, the applicant's action, the observed result or response, and the recommender's interpretation. Explain enough technical or disciplinary context for the intended reader to understand the contribution.
- Keep individual contribution and team results accurate. Preserve distinctions among preparation, submission, acceptance, and publication, and between completed and proposed work.
- Connect observed performance to the relevant level of academic study or research. Specific experience can support an inference about readiness. A prediction should reflect the strength and limits of that experience.
- Preserve the recommender's supplied position on strengths, growth, and reservations. Include developmental feedback when relevant to the prompt or the recommender's account. Use concrete evidence to express support.
- Tailor the recommendation to the target and supplied criteria. Keep claims about the target institution, faculty, funding, or the recommender's personal connections grounded in the provided or verified information.
- Close with a clear recommendation consistent with the evidence and stated stance. Include supplied name, title, institution, and contact details in a plain signature block when appropriate. Use placeholders for essential missing details and identify them outside the prose.
- Put editing notes, source notes, and questions outside the letter. In applicant-prepared drafts, reserve personal recollections, comparisons, and endorsement wording for the named recommender to confirm during review.

### Stage 4 Polish

Check whether the letter reads as a coherent professional evaluation.

1. Confirm names, relationship, dates, titles, grades, contribution, outcome metrics, publication status, and target against the available material. Check every phrase implying direct observation, long acquaintance, or comparative standing.
2. Check that evaluative claims follow from the examples. Distinguish a reasonable judgment about demonstrated work from a new event, result, or personal observation invented during drafting.
3. Match the requested voice and editing scope. Use a supplied writing sample for supported stylistic preferences while retaining this recommender's facts and judgment. Check third-person references to the applicant and first-person references to the recommender throughout.
4. Follow the supplied word, character, page, and questionnaire limits. Measure the actual text with suitable tools when an exact count is reported. Rendered files are needed to verify page layout.
5. Present the full requested draft. Keep any essential unresolved details in a short separate note and offer concrete questions needed to finish them.

### Stage 5 Iterate

Revise around the feedback and preserve confirmed facts.

1. Incorporate corrections from the applicant or recommender, updating all affected passages and target variants. Preserve the recommendation strength unless the recommender's evaluation or the user's authorized revision changes it.
2. For several recommenders, use the relationships and observed evidence to give each letter its own emphasis, organization, and known voice. Keep shared applicant facts consistent and track which recommender can discuss each episode.
3. For several target programs, adapt the audience, stated criteria, and concluding fit using supported information. Recheck institution names and opportunity details across versions.
4. Provide revised letters or files when requested using the host's available tools. Sending a recommendation, accessing a recommender's account, or submitting a reference requires its own explicit instruction and applicable authorization.

### Language and Related Work

Use the user's requested language for the letter and their preferred language for discussion. When the letter language is unclear, ask briefly or state a working assumption and proceed when useful. For bilingual output, preserve factual scope, personal knowledge, comparison, and recommendation strength in both versions. Follow the user's requested parallel or separate format.

When available, inquiry-email can help write a request to a recommender and application-review can check consistency across the supplied application materials. This skill can produce the recommendation independently using the available information.
